BNBC 2020 · Structural systems

Choosing R, and why a lower one is not automatically safer

Table 6.2.19 does not offer a factor to choose. It offers a system, its detailing, and the three numbers that come with it — and some of the low numbers are not permitted at all.

R is not a dial

BNBC 2020 Table 6.2.19 does not give you a response reduction factor to choose. It gives you a triplet — R, the overstrength factor Ω0, and the deflection amplification factor Cd — attached to a named structural system with named detailing requirements.

Taking R from one row and detailing to another is not a conservative simplification. It is a different building from the one the table describes.

The concrete rows engineers actually use

NL means no height restriction; NP means the system is not permitted in that Seismic Design Category.

SystemR Ω0Cd SDC BSDC CSDC D
Special RC moment frames 8.03.05.5 NLNLNL
Intermediate RC moment frames 5.03.04.5 NLNLNP
Ordinary RC moment frames 3.03.02.5 NLNPNP
Dual, special RC shear walls 7.02.55.5 NLNLNL
Dual, ordinary RC shear walls 6.02.55.0 NLNLNP

Why a lower R can be the wrong answer

The instinct is reasonable: a lower R gives a higher base shear, and a higher base shear feels safer. Read the table again.

An ordinary reinforced concrete moment frame is NP in SDC C and SDC D. Choosing it there is not a cautious choice of a smaller R. It is a system the Code does not permit at all, and no amount of extra base shear makes it permitted.

The same trap sits one row down: a dual system with ordinary RC shear walls is NP in SDC D, while the special version has no height restriction anywhere. The lower number is the forbidden one.

Our calculators enforce this rather than reporting it. Picking a system that is NP for the Seismic Design Category is refused, because printing a base shear for a building that cannot be built that way is worse than printing nothing.

The dual-system condition

Table 6.2.19 note 2: in a dual system the moment frames must be capable of resisting at least 25 per cent of the design forces. A frame-and-wall building is not automatically a dual system; it is one only if the frames carry their share.

This is checked after analysis, not assumed before it. If the walls take 85 per cent of the storey shear, the R you assigned from group D or E was never the right one.
