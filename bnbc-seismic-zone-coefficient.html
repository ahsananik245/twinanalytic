Seismic zone coefficient Z, and the Sa floor most calculations miss

Four zone values, one line of definition, and a minimum that bites hardest on exactly the buildings whose designers least expect it.

The four zones

BNBC 2020 Table 6.2.14 gives the seismic zone coefficient Z. There are four zones and four values, and nothing is interpolated between them:

ZoneZ
Zone 10.12
Zone 20.20
Zone 30.28
Zone 40.36

Z on its own is not a design quantity. It feeds the design spectral acceleration Sa, together with the importance factor I, the response reduction factor R, the site coefficients and the building period.

The floor that gets missed

Under Eq 6.2.34, Sec 2.5.4.3 defines Sa as the design spectral acceleration “which shall not be less than 0.67 β Z I”, with a recommended β of 0.11.

Sa ≥ 0.67 × 0.11 × Z × I

It is a single line in the middle of a definition rather than a numbered equation of its own, which is exactly why it is the provision most often left out of a spreadsheet. It is also not academic: the computed spectral value falls as the building period rises and as R rises, so the floor bites precisely on the tall, ductile, well-detailed buildings whose designers are least likely to expect a minimum to apply.

A spreadsheet that omits it does not fail loudly. It simply returns a base shear that is too low, for the buildings where being too low matters most.

The other one-line limit in the same clause

Sec 2.5.4.3 also states that the ratio I/R cannot be greater than one. In practice this rules out combining a high importance factor with a low-R system, which is a combination that otherwise looks conservative and is not permitted.

Working it through

Take a Dhaka site in Zone 2 (Z = 0.20) with an ordinary occupancy, I = 1.0. The floor is:

Sa,min = 0.67 × 0.11 × 0.20 × 1.0 = 0.01474

Any computed Sa below 0.01474 g is replaced by that value. Our seismic calculator reports both the computed figure and the floor side by side, and states which one governed, so the check is visible rather than assumed.
