Worked Example

6-Storey RC Residential Frame, Dhaka

This is not a client project. It is a design pass on a hypothetical building, published so you can see how we work before deciding whether to engage us.

Every figure on this page was produced by the free calculators on this site. Every input is listed, so you can enter the same numbers and get the same answers. Where a result depends on something we would normally take from an analysis model, we say so and stop rather than inventing it.

01The building

A regular six-storey reinforced concrete residential building on a flat site in Dhaka. The frame is deliberately ordinary: no transfer structure, no setbacks, no podium. An irregular building is a more interesting problem, but a regular one makes the method visible.

Storeys6
Storey height3.0 m
Total height, H18.0 m
Plan dimensions24.0 × 18.0 m
Lateral systemSpecial RC moment frame (R = 8)
Concrete, f′c25 MPa
Reinforcement, fy415 MPa
LocationDhaka

Why a special moment frame. A special frame (R = 8) is permitted in every seismic design category, so choosing it avoids a category-dependent argument about whether an intermediate frame would be allowed. It also means the detailing rules used later are the strict ones.

05Which case governs

Seismic1 312.2 kN
Wind760.7 kN
Ratio1.73

Seismic governs, by a factor of about 1.7. That is the usual outcome for a mid-rise concrete building in Dhaka: the mass is high, the building is short enough that wind pressure has not yet built up, and Zone 2 demand is significant. It would not stay true for a taller or much lighter structure, which is why both are checked rather than assumed.

06Beam design — ACI 318

A typical interior floor beam on a 6.0 m span with 4.5 m tributary width, designed with the beam design calculator. Both ends continuous, special moment frame detailing.

Loads on the beam

Superimposed dead, 7.00 kN/m² × 4.5 m31.50 kN/m
Beam self-weight, added by the tool at 24 kN/m³4.32 kN/m
Live, 2.0 kN/m² × 4.5 m9.00 kN/m
Factored, wu = 1.2(31.50 + 4.32) + 1.6(9.00)57.38 kN/m

A trap worth naming. The calculator adds the beam's own weight from the section dimensions. Entering a dead load that already includes the beam counts it twice — here that would have inflated Mu from 187.8 to 205.5 kN·m, about 9 %. The 1.50 kN/m² smeared frame allowance used for seismic weight is a global figure and is deliberately not reused for the local beam load.

Results

Flexural DCR0.80

With the interior-span coefficient wuL²/11, Mu = 57.38 × 6.0² / 11 = 187.8 kN·m, which matches the tool exactly. A 300 × 600 section with four 20 mm bars gives φMn = 234.3 kN·m. The strain of 0.0138 is comfortably past 0.005, so the section is tension controlled and φ = 0.90 — the ductile failure mode you want. Five bars were also checked and work, but four are enough; the extra bar buys nothing except cost and congestion.

Shear is not close to critical. The ∅10 stirrups at 125 mm centres are set by the special moment frame hoop spacing rules rather than by the shear demand — in a seismic frame the detailing usually governs the ends of the beam, not the force.

07Where this example stops

A real project carries on from here, and the next steps need a three-dimensional analysis model:

  • Storey drift and P-delta — need displacements from the model.
  • Column design — needs the axial-moment pairs each load combination produces, not a single assumed load. Part 2 takes the columns as far as tributary area honestly allows, which is preliminary sizing.
  • Torsional and stiffness irregularity — need storey stiffnesses and per-corner drifts.
  • Foundations — need column reactions and a geotechnical report.

The calculators for all of those are on this site, and we could have filled them with plausible-looking numbers. We have not, because invented displacements would make this page look more complete while making it worth less. The point of a worked example is that every figure can be checked.
